Why Gilbert Homes Experience Water Damage

Gilbert's rapid residential growth over the past two decades has reshaped how water moves through the town. New rooftops, driveways, and roadways have replaced open desert that once absorbed rainfall, and that shift shows up fastest during monsoon storms, when intense summer bursts can trigger flash flooding across newer subdivisions before drainage systems can keep pace.

Gilbert's extensive canal and irrigation infrastructure, much of it tied to the historic irrigation networks that shaped the town's agricultural roots, adds a layer of moisture management that many other East Valley cities don't share. Combined with our region's caliche soil, which absorbs water slowly and sends runoff pooling toward the nearest low point, and the near-universal slab-on-grade construction used throughout Gilbert, homes here face a distinct mix of risk factors.

Inside the home, the most common causes we see are familiar ones: water heater failures, appliance leaks, and HVAC condensate overflows during Gilbert's long, hot cooling season. In the town's older pockets, particularly near the Heritage District, aging plumbing adds another layer of risk that newer master-planned communities don't face in the same way.

It's worth noting that Gilbert slopes gently east-to-west, and some areas can experience stormwater accumulation during heavy monsoon events as a result. Gilbert also maintains extensive flood-control and retention infrastructure specifically to manage this runoff, which helps — but doesn't eliminate — the risk to individual homes during a major storm.

Gilbert Flood Risks Most Homeowners Don't Know About

Monsoon Season

Running June through September, sudden heavy rainfall can trigger street flooding and wash overflow risks with very little warning.

Caliche Soil Conditions

Low water absorption drives fast runoff, often leading to water pooling around foundations after even a short, intense storm.

Irrigation-Related Damage

Many Gilbert neighborhoods still use flood-irrigation systems tied to historic canal networks, creating unique moisture and landscaping challenges near the home.

Gilbert's canal and drainage corridors, including infrastructure connected to the broader Eastern Maricopa Floodway, along with local retention basins and other flood-control infrastructure, are designed to move stormwater away from neighborhoods efficiently. These systems represent a serious, ongoing flood management effort by the town — but during the heaviest monsoon cells, water can still outpace even well-designed infrastructure, which is when homes closest to these corridors are most at risk.

Will My Insurance Cover Water Damage?

Typically covered under a standard homeowners policy: burst pipes, water heater failures, appliance failures, and storm-created openings that let water in. Usually not covered: damage from long-term neglect, deferred maintenance, or groundwater flooding without flood insurance in place.

Gilbert participates in the National Flood Insurance Program, and residents can purchase flood insurance through NFIP for coverage that standard homeowners policies typically exclude. We help document your loss with photos and moisture logs to support whichever type of claim applies to your situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can you arrive in Gilbert?

Our crews are dispatched immediately after your call and respond throughout Gilbert as quickly as possible, with equipment already stocked and ready for extraction and drying.

How fast can mold grow after water damage?

Mold can begin developing within 24 to 48 hours in Arizona's warm indoor environments, which is why fast extraction and drying are so important after any water loss.

Can water damaged drywall be saved?

It depends on the water category and how long it sat. Clean water exposure for a short time may allow drying and saving the drywall, while contaminated or long-standing water often requires replacement.

Will insurance pay for restoration?

In most cases, yes, if the damage is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe or appliance failure. Groundwater flooding typically requires separate flood insurance through the NFIP.

What is moisture mapping?

Moisture mapping uses meters and thermal imaging to locate hidden water behind walls, under flooring, or inside cavities that aren't visible, helping guide the drying process and document progress for insurance.

How long does structural drying take?

Most residential structural drying takes three to five days with commercial equipment running continuously, though larger or more severe losses can take longer.

What should I do before your team arrives?

Shut off the water source and power to affected areas if it's safe, document the damage with photos, and avoid removing materials yourself so the restoration and insurance documentation stay accurate.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Standard homeowners policies typically exclude groundwater and surface flooding. Gilbert residents can purchase separate flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program for that type of coverage.
