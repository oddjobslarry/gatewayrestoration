Why Water Damage Happens So Often in Tempe

Tempe's water damage calls come from a mix of causes that don't line up neatly with a single housing type. Aging plumbing infrastructure in older homes near Downtown Tempe means galvanized pipe and decades-old fittings are more likely to fail without warning. Many of these homes were built well before today's plumbing codes, and small leaks behind walls can go unnoticed until damage is already significant.

Layered on top of that is Tempe's apartment and condo density. With so many multi-story, multi-unit buildings packed into a relatively small footprint, a single failure — a supply line, a water heater, an overflowing tub — can affect several units at once as water travels down through floor assemblies and shared walls. Student housing turnover adds another layer: as tenants move in and out each semester, appliances get more use, maintenance requests are sometimes delayed, and units can sit unoccupied for stretches where a slow leak isn't caught early.

On the mechanical side, water heater failures, appliance leaks, and HVAC condensate line overflows are constant contributors, especially during Tempe's long cooling season when AC systems run almost continuously and condensate lines are prone to clogging. Roof leaks during severe storms round out the list, particularly on older flat-roof apartment buildings and commercial structures that weren't built to handle today's more intense monsoon rain events.

The result is a city where commercial building water losses and residential losses happen side by side, often in the same block — which is exactly why Tempe's mix of older neighborhoods and modern multi-story developments creates restoration challenges you don't see the same way in a purely single-family-home market like parts of Chandler or Gilbert.

Flood Awareness

Tempe Flood Risks Homeowners and Property Managers Should Understand

Monsoon Storms

From July through September, sudden intense rainfall regularly causes street flooding and parking structure flooding across Tempe's dense commercial corridors.

Tempe Town Lake & Salt River Corridor

Tempe Town Lake functions as part of the broader Salt River flood-control system, designed to manage significant flood events while protecting surrounding development. This flood-control infrastructure plays a major role in how stormwater ultimately moves through Tempe during heavy rain.

Urban Runoff

Large paved surfaces across parking lots and commercial developments accelerate stormwater movement, sending more water toward buildings faster than undeveloped desert terrain would.

Flash Flood Conditions

The Valley has a long history of sudden desert storms overwhelming drainage systems in a matter of minutes, which is why properties near washes and low-lying streets face elevated flash flood risk during monsoon season.

Our Process

The Gateway Restoration 5-Step Process

Every Tempe job follows IICRC procedures, with moisture documentation and insurance-ready reporting built in from the first visit.

  • Emergency Inspection & Damage Assessment

    We assess the source, water category, and scope of damage, including checking adjacent units in multi-family buildings.

Insurance Guidance

Will Insurance Cover Water Damage?

Typically Covered

  • Burst pipes from sudden mechanical failure
  • Water heater failures
  • Appliance failures, such as a washing machine hose bursting
  • Sudden accidental discharge from plumbing systems
  • Storm-created openings, such as wind-driven rain through a damaged roof

Usually Not Covered

  • Long-term neglect of a known issue
  • Deferred maintenance on aging plumbing or appliances
  • Groundwater flooding without a separate flood insurance policy

We support every claim with thorough documentation: moisture logs, photos, and direct adjuster coordination, so your claim moves faster whether you're a homeowner, landlord, or property manager filing on behalf of a unit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly can you arrive in Tempe?

Our crews typically arrive within a couple of hours of your call, day or night. We stage response throughout the East Valley to keep arrival times fast for ASU-area rentals, apartments, and commercial properties.

Do you handle apartment and condo water damage?

Yes. We regularly restore water damage in apartment communities and condominiums throughout Tempe, coordinating with property managers, HOAs, and tenants to limit disruption.

Can water damage spread between units?

Yes. In multi-family buildings, water often migrates vertically through shared walls, floor assemblies, and utility chases, so we inspect adjacent and below units even when only one unit reported the leak.

How fast can mold grow in Tempe?

Mold can begin colonizing damp materials within 24 to 48 hours in Tempe's warm indoor environments, especially in older buildings with limited ventilation or units where a leak went unnoticed.

What is structural drying?

Structural drying removes moisture from a building's framing, drywall, and subfloor using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers, guided by IICRC drying calculations and verified with moisture readings.

Will insurance pay for restoration?

Most policies cover sudden, accidental water damage such as a burst pipe or water heater failure. Damage from long-term neglect or groundwater flooding without flood insurance is typically excluded. We help document your claim.

Do you work with property managers?

Yes. We regularly work directly with property management companies overseeing Tempe apartment communities and student housing, providing rapid response and detailed reporting.

Can you restore commercial buildings?

Yes. We restore office buildings, restaurants, retail spaces, medical facilities, and hospitality properties throughout Tempe, with scheduling built around minimizing downtime.

Do you provide emergency service at night?

Yes, Gateway Restoration provides 24/7 emergency water damage response throughout Tempe, including nights, weekends, and holidays.

What should I do before your technicians arrive?

Shut off the water source if it's safe to do so, avoid contact with standing water near electrical outlets, move valuables away from the affected area if possible, and take photos of the damage for your records.
