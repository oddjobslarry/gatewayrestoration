What To Do in the First 60 Minutes After Water Damage in Your Chandler Home

The first hour after you discover water intrusion determines how much of your home and your budget you protect. Chandler's dry climate means most homeowners aren't used to standing water, so it's easy to freeze up. Here's exactly what to do.

Stop the source

Shut off the main water valve if it's a plumbing failure, or the water heater supply line if that's the source. If the water is coming from a roof leak during a monsoon, that step waits until the storm passes.

Cut power to the affected area

If water is near outlets, appliances, or the electrical panel, shut off power to that zone at the breaker before doing anything else.

Call a Chandler water damage restoration company

Call (480) 591-8997. Every hour water sits raises the risk of drywall failure, warped flooring, and mold growth in Arizona's warm indoor environments.

Move valuables and furniture

Lift rugs, move electronics, and elevate furniture legs off wet flooring where it's safe to do so.

Document everything

Take photos and video of the water source, affected rooms, and damaged belongings before cleanup begins — this protects your insurance claim.

Avoid DIY drying with fans alone

Household fans can't reach the moisture trapped inside drywall, baseboards, and subfloor the way commercial equipment can, and incomplete drying is the leading cause of hidden mold.

The 5-Step Water Damage Restoration Process We Use in Chandler Homes

Step 1: Emergency Assessment and Water Category Classification

Every job starts with classifying the water. Category 1 Water is clean water from a supply line or appliance with no contamination risk. Category 2 Water ("gray water") contains some contaminants, such as water from a washing machine or dishwasher overflow, and carries a mild health risk. Category 3 Water is grossly contaminated — sewage backups, flood water, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria — and requires full protective protocols. Classification determines every step that follows.

Step 2: Water Extraction

Our crews use truck-mounted extraction equipment to remove standing water quickly, pulling far more volume and moisture than portable shop-vacs. This is the step that determines how much of your flooring and subfloor can be saved rather than replaced.

Step 3: Structural Drying

Once standing water is gone, we place air movers to accelerate evaporation from walls, floors, and framing, paired with commercial dehumidifiers that pull the moisture out of the air before it resettles. Equipment placement follows IICRC calculations based on room size, humidity, and material type — not guesswork.

Step 4: Moisture Monitoring and Documentation

We track drying progress daily using moisture mapping and thermal imaging to find hidden pockets of water inside walls and under flooring that aren't visible to the eye. Every reading is logged and included in your insurance documentation, so your adjuster sees objective proof the structure is dry — not just our word for it.

Step 5: Restoration and Rebuild

Once moisture readings confirm the structure is fully dry, we move into rebuild: replacing drywall, flooring, baseboards, and paint as needed to return your home to its pre-loss condition.

Chandler-Specific Water Damage Risks You Need To Know

Chandler homes face a different set of risks than homes in wetter climates, shaped by local soil, weather, and construction. Our region's caliche soil — a hard, cement-like layer beneath the topsoil — doesn't drain well, so water from a slab leak or irrigation failure has nowhere to go but up into your foundation. Expansive clay soil in some neighborhoods shifts as it absorbs moisture, stressing slab foundations and plumbing lines and contributing to slab leaks.

Monsoon storms between June and September bring intense, fast-moving rain that overwhelms roofs, gutters, and grading in a matter of minutes, while flash flooding can back up against homes near washes and low-lying streets. Arizona's heavy reliance on air conditioning also means HVAC condensate leaks are a common, often-overlooked source of interior water damage, especially in attics above bedrooms.

Chandler's hard water accelerates corrosion inside older galvanized plumbing, raising the odds of a pinhole leak or full pipe failure. And because so many Chandler homes are finished in stucco, wall moisture can get trapped behind the exterior coating long enough to invite mold before a homeowner ever notices a stain.

Will My Chandler Homeowners Insurance Cover This?

Most Chandler homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental damage — a burst pipe, a water heater failure, an appliance leak, or storm-related water intrusion from a monsoon or roof failure. These events happen without warning, which is exactly what standard policies are designed to cover.

What's typically excluded is damage tied to long-term neglect, deferred maintenance, or a gradual leak that went unaddressed for weeks or months — insurers expect homeowners to catch and fix slow leaks before they cause major damage. Many policies also carry specific mold exclusions or sub-limits, especially when mold results from an unresolved leak.

We work directly with your insurance adjuster, provide the moisture logs and photo documentation needed to support your claim, explain how your deductible applies, and can arrange direct billing with many carriers so you aren't covering the full cost upfront.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast should water damage be addressed?

Water damage should be addressed within the first few hours of discovery. Materials like drywall, carpet padding, and subfloor begin absorbing moisture immediately, and in Arizona's warm indoor environments, mold can begin developing within 24 to 48 hours. The faster extraction and drying begin, the more of your home and belongings can be saved.

Will insurance pay?

In most cases, yes, if the damage is sudden and accidental — such as a burst pipe, water heater failure, or storm intrusion. Damage caused by long-term neglect or a gradual leak is typically excluded. We help document your claim and work directly with your adjuster to support coverage.

Can mold grow in 24 hours?

Yes. In Arizona's warm indoor temperatures, mold spores can begin colonizing damp materials within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure. This is why immediate extraction and structural drying are so important, rather than waiting to see if an area dries on its own.

How long does drying take?

Most residential structural drying takes three to five days with commercial air movers and dehumidifiers running continuously. Larger losses, Category 3 water, or homes with dense materials like tile and concrete slab can take longer. We monitor daily with moisture readings to confirm when drying is complete.

What is Category 3 water?

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated water, such as sewage backups, flood water, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria. It poses a health risk and requires full protective equipment, aggressive removal of affected porous materials, and thorough sanitization during restoration.

Can my belongings be saved?

Many belongings can be saved if they're treated quickly. We offer content pack-out, document drying, and contents cleaning services to restore furniture, electronics, and important papers whenever possible, though some items exposed to Category 3 water may need to be discarded for safety.

Do I need to leave my house?

Not always. Many Category 1 and Category 2 water losses can be dried while you remain in the home, though certain rooms may be inaccessible during drying. Larger losses, sewage contamination, or extensive mold growth may require temporary relocation, which we'll walk you through during the assessment.

What does water mitigation mean?

Water mitigation refers to the immediate steps taken to stop water damage from getting worse — extraction, structural drying, and moisture control — before full restoration and rebuild work begins. Mitigation is what protects your home from secondary damage like mold and structural decay.

How much does restoration cost?

Costs typically range from $1,200 for minor Category 1 losses to $12,000 or more for extensive Category 3 damage, with mold remediation adding $1,500 to $6,000 if needed. Actual pricing depends on square footage, materials, and how long the water sat before treatment.

What should I do first?

Stop the water source if it's safe to do so, shut off power to any affected outlets, and call a Chandler water damage restoration company immediately. Document the damage with photos before cleanup begins, and avoid relying on household fans alone to dry the area.
