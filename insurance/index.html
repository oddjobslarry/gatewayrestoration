Understanding The Roles

Insurance vs. Restoration: Understanding the Difference

One of the most common points of confusion after water damage is who does what. Insurance carriers and restoration companies play completely different roles, and understanding that difference helps you know what to expect from each.

Your Insurance Company

Your carrier is responsible for the financial and contractual side of your claim.

  • Evaluates what your policy covers
  • Reviews policy language, endorsements, and exclusions
  • Assigns an adjuster to inspect the loss
  • Approves, denies, or partially approves portions of a claim
  • Issues payment based on your coverage and deductible

Gateway Restoration

We are responsible for the physical mitigation and restoration of your property.

  • Removes standing water from the property
  • Dries structural materials using commercial equipment
  • Documents moisture levels and drying progress
  • Prevents secondary damage such as mold growth
  • Provides restoration and rebuild services

Gateway Restoration does not evaluate your policy, does not decide what is covered, and does not have authority to approve or deny any part of your claim. Those decisions belong exclusively to your insurance carrier.

Coverage Basics

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Arizona?

Most Arizona homeowners policies are written to cover water damage that is sudden and accidental — meaning it happened without warning and wasn't the result of a problem the homeowner knew about and failed to address. Whether a specific loss is covered always depends on your individual policy, its endorsements, and any exclusions your carrier has applied.

✔ Often Covered

  • Burst pipes
  • Sudden plumbing failures
  • Water heater failures
  • Appliance supply line failures
  • Accidental overflows (tubs, sinks, washing machines)
  • Storm-created roof openings
  • Other sudden and accidental water events

✘ Often Not Covered

  • Long-term or gradual leaks
  • Damage tied to deferred maintenance
  • Neglect of a known plumbing issue
  • General wear and tear
  • Known, unresolved damage from a prior event

Because coverage varies significantly by carrier, policy form, and endorsement, the only way to know exactly what your policy covers is to read your declarations page and speak directly with your agent or carrier. For general guidance on homeowners insurance in Arizona, the Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions publishes consumer resources that explain how residential policies typically work.

A Critical Distinction

Flood Damage vs. Water Damage: Why the Difference Matters

Homeowners often use "flood" and "water damage" interchangeably, but insurance carriers treat them as two entirely different types of loss — and that difference can determine whether a claim is covered at all.

Water Damage

Typically originates from inside the home and is often included under a standard homeowners policy when the cause is sudden and accidental.

  • Burst pipes
  • Appliance failures
  • Plumbing leaks
  • Water heater failures

Flood Damage

Involves water entering from outside the structure and is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies.

  • Water entering from outside the home
  • Surface water accumulation
  • Ground-level flooding
  • Flash flooding

Chandler and the broader East Valley experience intense monsoon storms between June and September, which can produce flash flooding near washes and low-lying streets. When water enters a home from outside during a monsoon event, that loss is frequently classified as flood damage rather than water damage — meaning it may not be covered by a standard homeowners policy. Many Arizona property owners in flood-prone areas choose to carry a separate flood insurance policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood carrier, specifically to address this gap in standard coverage.

Step By Step

The Water Damage Insurance Claim Process

While every claim is different, most water damage claims in Chandler follow a similar general sequence from discovery to final completion.

1

Stop the Source

Shut off the main water valve or the specific supply line causing the leak, if it's safe to do so. Stopping active water flow is the first priority before anything else.

2

Document the Damage

Take photos and video of the affected areas, the water source, and any damaged belongings before cleanup begins. This documentation becomes an important part of your claim record.

3

Contact Your Insurer

Notify your insurance carrier to begin the claims process. Your carrier will provide a claim number and explain what information they'll need from you moving forward.

4

Begin Emergency Mitigation

A restoration company begins water extraction and emergency mitigation to prevent the damage from worsening. Most policies expect mitigation to start promptly rather than waiting for an adjuster.

5

Moisture Mapping and Drying

Structural drying begins using commercial air movers and dehumidifiers, with daily moisture readings logged to track progress and confirm when materials are dry.

6

Adjuster Review

Your insurance company's adjuster reviews the loss, often using the documentation and mitigation records provided by the restoration company, to determine coverage.

7

Repairs and Restoration

Once drying is confirmed complete and coverage is determined, repair and reconstruction work begins to return the property to its pre-loss condition.

8

Final Completion

A final walkthrough confirms all work is complete and the property has been restored, closing out the mitigation and restoration portion of the claim.

Water Classification

Water Damage Categories and Insurance Considerations

Every water loss is classified by contamination level, and that classification affects both the mitigation protocols required and, in many cases, how a claim is evaluated.

Category 1

Clean Water

Water from a clean source such as a supply line or appliance, with no contamination risk. This is typically the least complex category to mitigate and document.

Category 2

Gray Water

Water containing some contaminants, such as overflow from a washing machine or dishwasher, that carries a mild health risk if left untreated.

Category 3

Black Water

Grossly contaminated water, including sewage backups, flood water, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria. Requires full protective protocols.

Sewage backups and other Category 3 losses often require additional remediation steps beyond standard drying, including removal of affected porous materials and thorough sanitization. Because of the added scope, Category 3 losses are typically documented in greater detail to support the claim, and coverage for sewage backups sometimes requires a specific policy endorsement — another reason to confirm your coverage directly with your carrier.

Common Questions

Understanding Water Mitigation Charges

It's common for homeowners to be surprised by the line items on a mitigation invoice, especially if they've never been through a water loss before. Here's what those charges typically represent.

What Mitigation Charges Typically Cover

  • Emergency response and dispatch
  • Water extraction using truck-mounted or portable equipment
  • Dehumidification to remove moisture from the air
  • Air movers placed according to drying calculations
  • Daily monitoring and moisture readings
  • Moisture mapping and thermal imaging
  • Containment to prevent cross-contamination
  • Photo, video, and written documentation

Why Equipment Costs What It Does

Commercial-grade air movers and industrial dehumidifiers are significantly more powerful — and more expensive to purchase, maintain, and run — than household fans or box store dehumidifiers. They're sized and placed according to IICRC drying calculations specific to the room's dimensions and material types, not guesswork, which is what allows a structure to be confirmed dry in days rather than weeks.

Spotting Questionable Billing

Legitimate restoration invoices should clearly itemize equipment used, days of operation, labor performed, and materials involved, and should be backed by daily documentation. Be cautious of vague line items, charges that can't be tied to specific work performed, or pressure to sign broad authorization documents before you understand what you're agreeing to.

Homeowner Concerns

Water Damage Insurance Questions We Hear Most Often

Will insurance pay for mold?

It depends on the cause. Mold that develops directly from a sudden, covered water event is often included, sometimes subject to a mold sub-limit written into the policy. Mold tied to long-term moisture, deferred maintenance, or a previously known leak is frequently excluded. Reviewing your policy's mold provisions with your agent is the only way to know for certain.

Will insurance replace drywall?

Drywall damaged by a covered water event is commonly included in a claim, since removing and replacing saturated drywall is often necessary to properly dry the wall cavity and prevent mold. The specific scope depends on your adjuster's assessment and your policy terms.

Will insurance cover flooring?

Flooring damaged by a sudden, covered water event is commonly part of a claim, but coverage can vary by material type and whether the flooring was already showing wear prior to the loss. Documentation of the flooring's condition immediately after the event helps support this part of the claim.

Will insurance pay for hotel costs?

Some policies include "loss of use" or "additional living expense" coverage that can reimburse temporary housing costs if a covered loss makes your home uninhabitable during repairs. This coverage, and its limits, varies by policy — check your declarations page or ask your agent.

Will insurance cover slab leaks?

A sudden slab leak is often covered as a sudden and accidental plumbing failure, though some policies apply specific sub-limits or requirements for slab leak claims, and coverage can depend on whether the leak was discovered promptly. This is another area where policy language matters significantly.

What happens if the adjuster and contractor disagree?

Disagreements over scope or pricing between an adjuster and a restoration contractor do happen. In these situations, additional documentation, moisture readings, or a supplemental inspection can sometimes resolve the difference. For disputes that can't be resolved directly, homeowners may want to consult their policy's appraisal clause or seek guidance from a licensed public adjuster or attorney.

Local Factors

Arizona Water Damage Risks That Affect Claims

Chandler's climate, soil, and construction create a distinct set of water damage risks that show up repeatedly in local claims.

  • Monsoon storms — intense, fast-moving rain between June and September that overwhelms roofs and drainage
  • Flash flooding — sudden water intrusion near washes and low-lying streets
  • Caliche soil — a hard, poorly draining soil layer that traps water near foundations
  • Slab leaks — common in areas with expansive or shifting soil
  • AC condensate leaks — an often-overlooked source of interior water damage, especially in attics
  • Roof leaks — frequently tied to monsoon-driven wind and rain
  • Water heater failures — one of the most common sudden water losses in Chandler homes
  • Aging plumbing and foundation movement — corrosion and soil shifting that contribute to pinhole leaks and slab stress

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage in Arizona?

Most Arizona homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, water heater failure, or storm-related intrusion. Damage from long-term leaks, deferred maintenance, or neglect is typically excluded. Coverage always depends on the specific policy language and endorsements, so review your declarations page or ask your agent directly.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Generally, no. Standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood damage caused by water entering from outside the home, such as surface water or flash flooding. Flood losses usually require a separate flood insurance policy, often through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private flood carrier.

How much does water damage restoration cost in Chandler?

Costs typically range from $1,200 to $3,500 for Category 1 losses, $2,500 to $6,500 for Category 2, and $4,500 to $12,000 or more for Category 3. Mold remediation, if needed, typically adds $1,500 to $6,000 or more. Actual pricing depends on square footage, materials, and how long water sat before treatment.

What documentation should I keep?

Keep photos and video of the damage before cleanup, moisture readings, drying logs, equipment records, daily progress reports, and restoration invoices. This documentation supports your claim and gives your adjuster objective evidence of the loss and the mitigation performed.

Should I wait for an adjuster before drying my property?

No. Most policies require homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, which means mitigation should begin promptly. Document the damage thoroughly first, then begin emergency mitigation. Waiting days for an adjuster often allows secondary damage like mold to develop.

Will insurance pay for mold remediation?

It depends on the policy and the cause of the mold. Mold that results directly from a sudden, covered water event is often included or covered up to a sub-limit. Mold from long-term moisture or neglect is frequently excluded. Review your policy's mold provisions carefully.

What if the leak happened slowly?

Gradual or long-term leaks are one of the most common reasons claims are denied or reduced. Insurers generally expect homeowners to identify and address slow leaks before they cause significant damage.

What is Category 3 water?

Category 3 water is grossly contaminated water, such as sewage backups, flood water, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria. It poses a health risk and requires full protective protocols and thorough sanitization.

How long does drying take?

Most residential structural drying takes three to seven days with commercial equipment running continuously. Larger losses, Category 3 water, or dense materials like tile and concrete can extend that timeline.

Do you work with insurance companies?

Yes. We provide the documentation, moisture logs, and photo records that carriers commonly request, and we communicate with adjusters about the mitigation and restoration work performed. We do not adjust claims or make coverage decisions.

Are you an insurance company?

No. Gateway Restoration is a restoration and mitigation company. We do not sell insurance policies, evaluate coverage, or adjust claims. Coverage decisions are made exclusively by your insurance carrier.

Can you guarantee claim approval?

No restoration company can guarantee claim approval, and any company that promises this should raise concern. Approval depends on your policy language, the cause of loss, and your carrier's review.

What if my claim is denied?

You can request a written explanation from your carrier, ask about the specific policy language cited, and consider requesting a re-inspection. For questions about your legal rights, consult a licensed attorney or contact the Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions.

What should I do immediately after water damage?

Stop the source of water if it's safe to do so, shut off power to affected outlets if water is nearby, photograph and video the damage, and contact a restoration company to begin mitigation. Notify your insurance carrier as soon as reasonably possible.

How fast can mold grow in Chandler?

In Arizona's warm indoor environments, mold spores can begin colonizing damp materials within 24 to 48 hours of water exposure, which is why prompt extraction and structural drying matter so much.
