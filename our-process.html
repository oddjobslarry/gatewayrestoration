Quick answer

Gateway Restoration follows a five-step, IICRC-based process: assess and classify the water, extract it, dry the structure, monitor moisture daily with documentation, then restore and rebuild. Call (520) 231-0926 — crews respond 24/7.

The 5-step process

Step 1: Emergency assessment and water category classification

Every job starts with classifying the water. Category 1 is clean water from a supply line or appliance. Category 2 (gray water) contains some contaminants, such as water from a washing machine or dishwasher overflow. Category 3 is grossly contaminated — sewage backups, flood water, or water that has sat long enough to grow bacteria — and needs full protective protocols. Classification determines every step that follows.

Step 2: Water extraction

Our crews use truck-mounted extraction equipment to remove standing water quickly, pulling far more volume and moisture than portable shop-vacs. This is the step that determines how much of your flooring and subfloor can be saved rather than replaced.

Step 3: Structural drying

Once standing water is gone, we place air movers to speed evaporation from walls, floors, and framing, paired with commercial dehumidifiers that pull moisture out of the air before it resettles. Equipment placement follows IICRC calculations based on room size, humidity, and material type — not guesswork.

Step 4: Moisture monitoring and documentation

We track drying progress daily using moisture mapping and thermal imaging to find hidden pockets of water inside walls and under flooring. Every reading is logged and included in your insurance documentation, so your adjuster sees objective proof the structure is dry.

Step 5: Restoration and rebuild

Once moisture readings confirm the structure is fully dry, we restore what was removed — drywall, flooring, trim, and paint — and return your home to its pre-loss condition.

Step 6: Prevention and final check

Before we leave, we confirm the moisture source is fixed or clearly identified, check that indoor humidity is back to a normal range, and walk you through simple steps to keep the problem from coming back.

What to do in the first 60 minutes

Stop the source if you can do so safely. Cut power to the affected area. Call a Chandler water damage restoration company. Move valuables and furniture out of the water. Document everything with photos. And avoid DIY drying with fans alone — it rarely dries inside walls, where mold starts. Read the full emergency water damage checklist.
