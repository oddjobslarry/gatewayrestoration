The Basics

What Is Category 1 Water?

Category 1 water is the term restoration professionals use for water damage that comes from a clean, sanitary source. Under IICRC guidelines, water is placed into one of three categories based on how contaminated it is at the moment it is released, not based on how long it has been sitting or where it ends up.

Category 1 water originates from sources like a broken supply line, a malfunctioning water heater, or an overflowing sink fed by a clean water line. At the moment of release, this water poses minimal health risk if contacted or accidentally consumed, which is why it is considered the least hazardous of the three categories.

It helps to separate two ideas that often get confused: water category and water class. Category describes contamination level, Category 1, 2, or 3. Class describes the amount of moisture absorbed and the rate of evaporation, which affects how much drying equipment and time a job requires. A Category 1 loss can still be a Class 3 loss if it soaks a large area quickly.

Quick Definition

Do Not Underestimate It

Why Clean Water Damage Is Still Serious

Clean water still causes real, sometimes extensive, structural damage, even when the contamination risk is low.

  • Water travels rapidly: clean water spreads across flooring and under walls just as fast as any other water loss
  • Drywall absorption: drywall wicks moisture upward and can lose structural integrity within hours
  • Cabinet swelling: particleboard cabinet bases swell and delaminate quickly when exposed to standing water
  • Flooring damage: hardwood cups, laminate swells, and tile grout lines can trap moisture underneath
  • Insulation saturation: wet insulation loses effectiveness and can hold moisture against framing for weeks
  • Hidden moisture: water inside wall cavities or under flooring often goes unnoticed until damage is advanced
Important Education

How Category 1 Water Becomes Category 2

Category 1 status is not permanent. Left untreated, clean water can deteriorate and become more contaminated over time.

Warm indoor temperatures, contact with carpet, drywall, or wood, and simple time all allow bacteria to multiply in standing water that started out clean. Water that sits for more than about 48 hours is generally considered to have progressed beyond Category 1, regardless of how clean it was at the start.

This is not a hypothetical risk. We regularly respond to Chandler vacation homes where a small supply line leak went unnoticed for days while the owners were away. We have seen weekend leaks behind a washing machine that were clean water Friday night and a much larger problem by Monday morning. Hidden wall leaks and unnoticed appliance failures follow the same pattern, the damage compounds the longer it goes unaddressed.

Chandler-Specific Water Damage Risks

Chandler's construction and climate shape how Category 1 losses behave here compared to other regions.

Slab-On-Grade Construction

Most Chandler homes sit on concrete slab, so water from a supply line leak has nowhere to drain and simply spreads.

Hidden Moisture Beneath Flooring

Tile and laminate installed directly over slab can trap moisture that is not visible until damage is advanced.

Hard Water Plumbing Issues

Mineral buildup in Arizona's hard water accelerates corrosion in supply lines and water heater connections.

Summer HVAC Operation

Heavy air conditioning use raises indoor humidity risk once water is already present in a structure.

Monsoon Season Humidity

Elevated outdoor humidity during monsoon months slows natural evaporation of an indoor leak.

Expansive Clay And Caliche Soils

Caliche layers beneath Chandler homes trap water rather than letting it disperse naturally underground.
