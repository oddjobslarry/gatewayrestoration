Local Causes

Why Water Heaters Fail in Chandler Homes

Chandler's water supply and climate are harder on water heaters than most homeowners realize. The same conditions that make Arizona a great place to live quietly shorten the working life of the tank sitting in your garage.

💧Hard Water & Mineral Buildup

Chandler's municipal water carries heavy mineral content. Over years of use, that mineral load settles inside the tank as scale and sediment, insulating the heating element from the water and forcing the unit to work harder while corroding the tank walls from the inside out.

🌡️Heat & Thermal Expansion

Garage-installed water heaters — the norm in most Chandler homes — regularly sit in triple-digit ambient heat through the summer. That heat accelerates thermal expansion inside the tank and stresses fittings, gaskets, and the pressure relief valve well beyond what a cooler climate would produce.

⚙️Aging Components

Pressure relief valves, expansion tanks, supply line connections, and shutoff valves all have a service life. Loose fittings and worn shutoff valves are among the most common sources of slow leaks that go undetected for months before finally letting go.

🔩Corrosion & Sediment

Sediment accumulation at the bottom of the tank traps moisture against the steel shell, creating the conditions for rust-through at the seams and the base — usually the first place a slow leak actually starts.

🏗️Expansion Tank Failures

The small expansion tank mounted near the water heater absorbs pressure spikes from thermal expansion. When its internal bladder fails, it can leak steadily or transfer excess pressure back into the main tank, accelerating wear on both.

🏘️Aging Housing Stock

Many Chandler neighborhoods built during the area's major growth periods now have original or first-replacement water heaters reaching the end of their expected service life at roughly the same time — which is why we see clusters of failures across similar-age homes each year.

⏳ Expected lifespan: Traditional tank water heaters last 8–12 years; tankless units last 15–20 years. If your unit is past that window, a leak isn't a matter of if — it's when.
Early Detection

Warning Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Leak

Most tank failures don't happen without warning — they announce themselves quietly for weeks before the leak becomes obvious. Catching these signs early can mean a simple repair instead of a full water heater flood cleanup.

  • Rust-colored staining or streaks around the base of the tank or on the floor beneath it.
  • Popping or rumbling noises during heating cycles, a sign of sediment buildup on the heating element or tank floor.
  • Water pooling under the tank, even a small amount, especially near the drain valve or base seams.
  • Visible corrosion on fittings, the tank shell, or the connections at the top of the unit.
  • Slow dripping at supply line or fitting connections, often mistaken for condensation.
  • Unexplained increases in your water bill, which can point to a leak inside the wall or slab feeding the unit.
  • Discolored or rusty hot water coming from your faucets, indicating internal tank corrosion.
  • Reduced hot water supply or water that cools faster than it used to.
  • Moisture or corrosion around the expansion tank mounted near the main unit.
  • Water discharging from the pressure relief valve or its overflow pipe, a sign of excess pressure.
  • Dampness inside the burner compartment on gas units, which can also point to condensation from a cracked heat exchanger.

If you notice two or more of these signs, don't wait for the tank to fail completely. A quick inspection now is far less disruptive than an emergency water heater flood cleanup later.

Act Fast

What To Do Immediately If Your Water Heater Starts Leaking

The first few minutes matter. Here's exactly what to do, in order, before help arrives.

1

Turn Off the Power

For electric water heaters, shut off the breaker dedicated to the unit. Never touch electrical components while standing in water.

2

Shut Off the Gas Supply (Gas Units)

Turn the gas valve at the unit to the "off" position to eliminate any fire or carbon monoxide risk while the leak is active.

3

Turn Off the Water Supply

Close the cold water shutoff valve above the tank. If it's stuck or you can't locate it, shut off the home's main water valve instead.

4

Protect Valuables Nearby

Move stored boxes, tools, and belongings out of the affected area and off the wet floor to limit secondary damage.

5

Photograph the Damage

Take clear photos and video of the leak source, the water spread, and any affected belongings before cleanup begins — this protects your insurance claim.

6

Call Gateway Restoration Immediately

7

Do Not Delay Drying

Household fans and towels can't reach moisture trapped inside cabinets, drywall, and subfloor. Delaying professional drying is the single biggest factor in whether mold develops.

Drying is only complete once readings confirm materials are back to a normal, dry standard — not just visually dry.

Can My Water Heater Damage Be Covered by Insurance?

In most cases, yes — but coverage depends heavily on how the leak happened.

✅ Usually Covered

  • Sudden, accidental tank ruptures
  • Complete burst tank failures
  • Sudden supply line failures
  • Unexpected overflow events

⚠️ Not Usually Covered

  • Damage from neglected maintenance
  • Long-term, slow seepage
  • Old, unresolved leaks left untreated
  • Damage attributed to normal wear and tear

Why It Matters

Why Fast Drying Matters

Every material in your home behaves differently once it's wet, and the clock starts the moment the leak begins.

Drywall

Wicks moisture upward and begins to break down structurally within hours, well before visible staining appears.

Cabinets

Particleboard cores swell and delaminate at the seams, often requiring full box replacement if drying is delayed.

Subfloor

Plywood and OSB subfloor can swell, warp, and lose structural strength when moisture is trapped beneath flooring.

Wood Framing

Saturated framing lumber is vulnerable to warping and, if left wet too long, decay that compromises structural integrity.

Insulation

Wet insulation loses its effectiveness and can hold moisture against framing long after surfaces appear dry.

Indoor Air Quality

Trapped moisture is the leading driver of mold growth, which can affect indoor air quality well beyond the original leak site.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a leaking water heater cause mold?

Yes. In Arizona's warm indoor temperatures, mold can begin developing on wet drywall, cabinetry, and flooring within 24 to 48 hours. Fast extraction and structural drying are the most effective way to prevent it.

Should I replace my flooring?

Not always. Tile and vinyl plank can often be dried and reused if water is removed quickly. Hardwood and engineered wood have a narrower window before cupping or delamination makes replacement necessary — the timeline of your call matters.

Can hardwood be saved?

In many cases, yes, if drying begins within the first several hours using specialized drying mats and controlled airflow. Once boards have cupped or the finish has separated, replacement is usually the more practical option.

Is a leaking water heater dangerous?

It can be. Water near electrical outlets or panels creates shock risk, and gas units carry a fire or carbon monoxide risk if the gas supply isn't shut off. Always cut power and gas before attempting anything else.

How much water can a failed tank release?

A standard 40 to 50-gallon residential tank can release its entire volume in minutes during a full rupture, and continued supply flow through a broken fitting can add many more gallons before the water is shut off.

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst water heater?

Most policies cover sudden, accidental failures such as a burst tank or supply line failure. Damage from long-term neglect or a slow, unresolved leak is typically excluded — documentation of the sudden nature of the event matters for your claim.

Can cabinets be dried?

Often yes, if toe kicks and cabinet interiors are opened quickly to allow airflow. Particleboard components that have already swollen or delaminated typically need to be replaced rather than dried.

Can drywall be saved?

Lower sections affected by clean water can sometimes be dried in place if treated quickly. Drywall that has wicked moisture high above the baseboard line or shows signs of breakdown is usually removed and replaced during restoration.

How long does drying take?

Most residential water heater leak jobs take three to five days of continuous drying with commercial air movers and dehumidifiers. Larger losses or dense materials like stone and concrete can take longer, and we confirm completion with daily moisture readings.

How soon should I call?

Immediately. Call (480) 591-8997 as soon as you discover the leak. Every hour of delay increases the risk of damage to flooring, cabinetry, drywall, and the likelihood of mold developing.
